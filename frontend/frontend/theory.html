Spacetime Geometry

In General Relativity, gravity is not treated as a conventional force. Mass and energy curve spacetime, and objects move along the geometry created by that curvature.

02.

Schwarzschild Metric

For a static, non-rotating, spherically symmetric black hole of mass M, spacetime is described by the Schwarzschild solution.

ds² = −(1 − 2M/r)dt² + (1 − 2M/r)⁻¹dr² + r²(dθ² + sin²θ dφ²)

The event horizon occurs at:

rₛ = 2M
03.

Geodesics

Particles and light follow geodesics — the straightest possible paths through curved spacetime.

d²xμ/dλ² + Γμ αβ (dxα/dλ)(dxβ/dλ) = 0

The Christoffel symbols encode how the coordinates respond to the underlying spacetime curvature.

04.

Photons & Null Geodesics

Photons move along null geodesics. Their spacetime interval therefore satisfies:

ds² = 0

This constraint is central to ray tracing because it determines how light bends, escapes, or becomes captured by the black hole.

05.

Conserved Quantities

Symmetries of the spacetime create conserved quantities along geodesics. In Schwarzschild spacetime, the most useful are energy and angular momentum.

Energy E = constant
Angular Momentum L = constant
06.

Effective Potential

The radial motion of particles and photons can be interpreted through an effective potential. Peaks and minima reveal unstable and stable orbit regions.

unstable photon orbit region

Characteristic Radii

Several radii define the important structures surrounding a black hole.

08.

Kerr Black Hole

The Kerr solution describes a rotating black hole. Rotation introduces frame dragging, an ergosphere, spin-dependent characteristic radii and asymmetric photon trajectories.

Outer Horizon r₊ = M + √(M² − a²)
Frame Dragging ω ≠ 0
Ergosphere Kerr only
